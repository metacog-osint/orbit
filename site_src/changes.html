Orbit changes

Changes in watched satellites' orbits, and the reference histories of rendezvous and proximity operations and anti-satellite tests.

Orbit changes in watched satellites, last 30 days

Source: Space-Track.org, United States Space Command (USSPACECOM), 18th Space Defense Squadron. Element sets are USSPACECOM's, redistributed under its blanket approval for basic space situational awareness data, which requires this citation. The orbit changes listed here are The Intelligence Company's own analysis of those element sets. They are not USSPACECOM's, and USSPACECOM and Space-Track have not reviewed, endorsed or checked them.

A listed change means a watched object's published orbit moved by more than its element sets usually move. It is not a confirmed manoeuvre, a statement of intent or a warning: a re-fit after a tracking gap, a single element set fitted off the orbit, a mislabelled element set or, in low orbit, storm-driven drag can do the same. Not for conjunction assessment, collision avoidance or any operational use. Satellite operators should use Space-Track's own services.

How Orbit marks a change: each pair of consecutive element sets is measured against that object's own usual set-to-set movement, after its steady drift is taken out. The change is given as the velocity it implies (along track, plane change, or orbit shape), which is a rough size, not a burn record. In geostationary orbit only changes of 0.25 m/s or more are listed. In geostationary orbit a change is listed only when the next element set confirms it, so it appears one set later (a median of about 9 to 13 hours on the records measured; as long as the next public set takes, for sparsely tracked objects). One set fitted off the orbit, which gives a change and its return, is counted and not listed. In low orbit, where plane and shape readings are noisiest, a change that is mainly a plane change or orbit shape is listed only at 1 m/s or more; each change also shows the velocity its semi-major-axis change alone implies. For a station-kept geostationary satellite held near zero inclination, the element sets in the days after a north-south burn often jump back and forth in plane, so one burn can be listed as several plane changes whose sum is two to four times what the burn needed. Changes less than a day apart are shown as one when they point the same way. A change measured across two days or more without an element set is labelled with that gap: it happened somewhere inside it. Quiet watched objects are counted, not named.

For a geostationary satellite the row also says what the element sets show: the longitude before and after, and the drift, which is how many degrees of longitude a day it moves along the belt (under 0.1° a day it is holding its place). A satellite above geostationary altitude circles slower than the Earth turns, so it falls back to the west; one below runs ahead to the east. How long it held the earlier longitude is counted only through the sets Orbit holds. A row marked notable is a satellite with a listed role on the GEO page whose drift rate changed by a quarter of a degree a day or more; it is not a warning. Other names for an object come from the TIC catalog; its own page says which source gives which.

Reference · Rendezvous and proximity operations in space

Every robotic rendezvous and proximity operation in Secure World Foundation's history sheet, in SWF's three tabs and SWF's own words: who, when, which satellite approached which, and what for. Orbit reads SWF's dates into a span and names the rows whose dates it cannot read; satellites are matched to GCAT only where exactly one GCAT payload fits. A record of past and planned operations, not new events and not Orbit's judgment of intent.

Reference · Anti-satellite tests in space, 1959 to date

Every anti-satellite test in Secure World Foundation's history sheet, as SWF compiled it: who tested, when, with which interceptor, against which target, and the cataloged debris created, since re-entered and still on orbit. A record of past tests, not new events; tests SWF adds are listed at the top after Orbit's next reading.
