Catalog

Changes to the public satellite catalog between Orbit's own readings, breakups, and the last 30 days counted against the two years before.

Catalog changes and owner codes

Changes between Orbit's last two readings of GCAT. Owners come from GCAT, which keeps Soviet (SU) objects apart from Russian (RU) ones.

Breakups and collisions, last 30 days

Objects GCAT marks exploded (E, broken up in orbit) or collided (C), with a date in the last 30 days. They left the catalog as one object without reentering, so they are kept apart from reentries. Pieces from them can later show as new catalog entries from an old launch (under Catalog changes). Dates are often approximate (marked ~). Orbit reports what GCAT records and does not say why an object broke up.

The last 30 days against the two years before

Counts from GCAT's own history: orbital launches by launching country and agency, payloads launched by owner, and reentries by owner, in the last 30 days and in each of the 24 windows of 30 days before them. "Above" or "below" means outside every one of those 24 windows. A count, not a warning. GCAT adds payloads and dates reentries as it learns of them, so the newest window for those two can still grow.
