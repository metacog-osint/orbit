    New to this? The terms used here
    NORAD number
    The catalog number the US Space Force gives each tracked object, as 25544 for the International Space Station. Objects without one are keyed by GCAT's own id, as A04907.
    COSPAR ID
    The international designator: launch year, launch number in that year and a letter for each piece, as 1998-067A.
    Perigee, apogee
    The lowest and highest points of an orbit, here in kilometres above the surface.
    Inclination
    The angle between the orbit and the equator: about 0° stays over the equator, 90° passes over the poles, above 90° runs against the Earth's turn.
    LEO, MEO, GEO
    Low Earth orbit, below about 2,000 km; medium Earth orbit, between that and geostationary; geostationary orbit, about 35,786 km above the equator, where a satellite keeps pace with the Earth's turn and stays over one longitude.
    Element set
    The published numbers that describe an orbit at one moment (its epoch). A path drawn from one drifts from the real one the further it runs from that moment.
    TIP
    Tracking and Impact Prediction: United States Space Command's reentry predictions on Space-Track.org, each a predicted time and a window either side of it.
    GCAT
    Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects.
    RPO
    Rendezvous and proximity operations: one spacecraft approaching or meeting another, as Secure World Foundation's public sheet lists them.
