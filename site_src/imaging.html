Imaging

The newest scene that a public catalogue lists over each launch site, from Landsat 8 and 9, Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1, and the days Landsat is next due over it.

Last acquired

The newest scene each catalogue lists whose footprint covers the place, searched over the last 32 days: USGS LandsatLook (Landsat Collection 2 Level-1) and the Copernicus Data Space catalogue (Sentinel-2 Level-1C, Sentinel-1 ground-range scenes). A catalogue lists a scene some hours after it is taken. Cloud is the scene's own estimate; Sentinel-1 is radar, so cloud does not apply. Other satellites, commercial and government, image these places too and mostly publish nothing, so a long gap here does not mean a place went unimaged.

Landsat next due

UTC days in the next 16 on which Landsat 8 or 9 flies a path that has covered the place in daylight in the last 32 days, on USGS's nominal 16-day acquisition calendar. USGS's calendar file runs to ; it repeats every 16 days, so Orbit carries it forward and checks every daylight scene it reads against it. A place with a scene off the calendar shows "unknown". Due is the calendar's path for the day, not a promise that a scene will be taken or will be clear.
